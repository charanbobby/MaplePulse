# MaplePulse — Marketing YouTube Script (Scenario Showcase)

**Target audience:** Marketers, brand managers, agency folks, product teams
**Tone:** Fast-paced, visual, "look what this can do" energy — demo-heavy
**Length:** ~8-10 minutes
**Format:** Each scenario is a quick 30-60s segment — type it in, show the panel, show reactions

---

## INTRO — What Is MaplePulse and Why Does It Exist? (2 min)

- "You've written a marketing message. Maybe it's an ad, a product launch, a survey, a government notice. Before you spend money putting it in front of real Canadians — how do you know it's going to land?"
- **The problem today:**
  - Traditional focus groups: $15-50K, weeks to organize, 8-12 people in a room in Toronto — and you hope they represent the country
  - Online surveys: low response rates, no nuance, people speed-click through them
  - Gut feeling: honestly, this is how most decisions get made — "I think this sounds good" and you ship it
  - The gap: there's nothing fast, cheap, and Canadian-specific to pressure-test your messaging before you commit budget
- **What MaplePulse is:**
  - A synthetic focus group for Canada — AI personas grounded in real Canadian data that react to your content in seconds
  - You describe your target audience in plain English — "Costco shoppers," "Muslim homebuyers in the GTA," "rural retirees in Nova Scotia" — and the system assembles a panel that matches
  - Each persona is built from 30+ real data sources: Statistics Canada census, Job Bank wage data, housing surveys, cultural demographics, regional concerns
  - They're not random AI characters — they're statistically grounded in how Canada actually looks
- **What MaplePulse is NOT:**
  - Not a replacement for real research, real customers, or real user interviews
  - Think of it as a spell-checker for your messaging — it catches blind spots, cultural missteps, and tone mismatches before you go live
  - A first filter, not the final answer
- **Who it's for:**
  - Marketers testing ad copy across provinces and cultures
  - Product managers validating concepts before building
  - Government and non-profits checking if messaging resonates outside the Ottawa bubble
  - Anyone who's ever shipped something and thought "I should have tested this with someone who isn't exactly like me"
- Transition: "Let me show you how it works."

---

## THE APP — What You're Looking At (1.5 min)

- "Before I throw scenarios at it, let me show you what MaplePulse actually is."
- **Screen: Landing page.** Clean UI, two input fields — that's it.
  - Top field: **Target Audience** — describe who you want to test against in plain English
  - Bottom field: **Content to Test** — paste your ad copy, product pitch, survey question, whatever
  - Hit Run.
- **What happens behind the scenes (keep it brief, not technical):**
  - The system parses your audience description and figures out the demographics — age, income, province, cultural background, occupation — all inferred from your plain-English brief
  - It assembles a panel of 12-20 AI personas, each grounded in real Canadian census data, wage data, and 30+ public data sources
  - Each persona reacts independently — and to reduce AI bias, multiple AI models evaluate the same persona (not just one model's opinion)
  - You get sentiment scores, cultural flags, tone fit, and a suggested rewrite
- **Show the three modes** (quick click-through):
  - **Focus Group** — one message, full panel reactions, optimization, before/after
  - **A/B Copy Test** — two or more variants, head-to-head with a winner picked
  - **Survey Pre-Test** — paste survey questions, get clarity/bias/ambiguity analysis
- **Show the filters panel** (open it, don't use it):
  - Province, sex, income, language, political leaning, age range, panel size slider
  - "You can manually filter if you want — but the whole point is you usually don't have to. The audience brief does the work."
- **Show an example persona card** — click into one reaction to show the persona's full profile:
  - Age, occupation, province, city, income, cultural background, languages, concerns, housing, commute
  - "Each of these is a synthetic person built from real statistical distributions — not a random AI hallucination."
- Transition: "Okay, now let's see how it handles real scenarios. I'm going to throw 15 at it."

## SCENARIO 1: Costco Shoppers — Minimal Input (45s)

**Audience brief:** `Costco shoppers`
**Message:** `Save big this weekend — stock up on essentials at unbeatable prices.`

- **What to show:** The system infers demographics from just "Costco shoppers" — age 30-65, household income $80K+, suburban families, bulk buyers, value-conscious homeowners
- **Point to make:** You typed two words and got a fully profiled panel. The system knows Costco's customer base and builds accordingly.
- **Look for in reactions:** Suburban parents responding differently than urban singles. Someone flagging that "unbeatable prices" feels generic. Costco members expecting specifics (what products? what aisle?).

## SCENARIO 2: Life Event — New Parents (45s)

**Audience brief:** `First-time parents in Ontario, babies under 12 months`
**Message:** `Introducing BabyBox Canada — everything your newborn needs, delivered monthly. Curated by Canadian pediatricians.`

- **What to show:** Panel skews 25-38, mixed income, Ontario-heavy. Both mothers and fathers on the panel.
- **Point to make:** Life events create hyper-specific audiences. "First-time parents" is a mindset, not just a demographic.
- **Look for in reactions:** Trust concerns ("who are these pediatricians?"), price sensitivity from lower-income parents, cultural differences in parenting approaches, someone asking "is this just another subscription box?"

## SCENARIO 3: Cultural — Diwali Campaign (45s)

**Audience brief:** `Diwali marketing campaign for a jewelry brand`
**Message:** `This Diwali, gift the brilliance she deserves. 22K gold collections inspired by tradition, crafted for today.`

- **What to show:** System detects "diwali" → sets holiday_or_festival, infers South Asian cultural background, pulls personas with matching cultural profiles
- **Point to make:** Cultural holidays aren't generic — the system matches the right community. A Diwali campaign gets South Asian Canadians, not random panelists.
- **Look for in reactions:** Gendered assumptions ("she deserves" — what about men buying for themselves?), authenticity flags ("inspired by tradition" might feel performative), regional differences between South Asian communities (Tamil vs. Punjabi vs. Bengali perspectives on gold jewelry)

## SCENARIO 5: Regional — Quebec French Ad (45s)

**Audience brief:** `Francophone Quebecers, 25-45, urban`
**Message:** `Découvrez notre nouvelle gamme de produits locaux — fait ici, pour vous.`

- **What to show:** Panel is Quebec-only, French-speaking, urban. Political leanings and cultural identity come through.

## SCENARIO 6: Immigration — Newcomer Services (45s)

**Audience brief:** `Recent immigrants to Canada, arrived in last 3 years, settling in Toronto or Vancouver`
**Message:** `Welcome to Canada. Open a no-fee bank account today — designed for newcomers, with support in your language.`

- **What to show:** Panel includes varied immigration backgrounds — South Asian, Chinese, Filipino, Middle Eastern, African. Different languages spoken.
- **Point to make:** "Newcomers" is not a monolith. A software engineer from India and a refugee from Syria have very different needs and reactions.
- **Look for in reactions:** Trust issues with banks. "Support in your language" — which language? Personas from different backgrounds flagging different concerns. Someone noting that "Welcome to Canada" might feel patronizing to someone who's been here 2 years.

## SCENARIO 8: Life Event — Retirement Planning (45s)

**Audience brief:** `Canadians aged 55-65, considering retirement in next 5 years, household income $80K+`
**Message:** `Ready to retire on your terms? Our advisors build personalized plans so you can enjoy what matters most.`

- **What to show:** Panel is older, higher income, mixed provinces. System includes relevant concerns: healthcare, housing, pension.
- **Point to make:** Life stage targeting. These aren't just "old people" — they're pre-retirees with specific financial anxieties.
- **Look for in reactions:** "On your terms" feels empty without specifics. Public sector workers with defined benefit pensions vs. private sector with RRSPs — very different needs. Someone in Alberta worried about healthcare portability if they move provinces.

## SCENARIO 9: Rural Canada — Broadband (45s)

**Audience brief:** `Rural Canadians in the Prairies and Atlantic provinces, concerned about internet access`
**Message:** `High-speed internet is coming to your community. Sign up today for our rural connectivity program.`

- **What to show:** Panel is rural, lower-density provinces — Saskatchewan, Manitoba, New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, PEI. Mix of farmers, tradespeople, small business owners.
- **Point to make:** Rural Canada is massively underrepresented in most market research. MaplePulse has personas there.
- **Look for in reactions:** Deep skepticism — "we've heard this before." Infrastructure concerns. Satellite vs. fibre debates. Someone asking about pricing, not just availability. A farmer noting that "high-speed" needs a number attached.

## SCENARIO 10: Gen Z — Fintech (45s)

**Audience brief:** `Gen Z urban professionals, 22-28, first real job, living in Toronto, Montreal, or Vancouver`
**Message:** `Ditch your parents' bank. WealthPilot saves, invests, and tracks your money — all from your phone.`

- **What to show:** Young panel, urban, mixed cultural backgrounds, lower income brackets (early career).
- **Point to make:** Tone matters enormously for Gen Z. "Ditch your parents' bank" could be edgy or cringe depending on who reads it.
- **Look for in reactions:** Some love the rebellious tone, some find it disrespectful (especially in cultures where parents manage family finances). "All from your phone" is table stakes, not a differentiator. Trust concerns — "why would I trust a startup with my money?"

## SCENARIO 12: Sustainability — EV Campaign (45s)

**Audience brief:** `Environmentally conscious Canadians, 30-50, considering switching to an electric vehicle, income $70K+`
**Message:** `Go electric this spring. $5,000 federal rebate + zero emissions = a smarter drive for Canada.`

- **What to show:** Mixed provinces — important because EV infrastructure varies dramatically. Income-qualified panel.

## SCENARIO 13: Healthcare — Telehealth (45s)

**Audience brief:** `Canadians frustrated with family doctor wait times, mix of urban and rural`
**Message:** `See a doctor in minutes, not months. Virtual care that fits your life — covered by your provincial plan.`

- **What to show:** Broad panel — this is a pain point that cuts across demographics. System picks up healthcare as a top concern.

## SCENARIO 14: A/B Test — Two Headlines (45s)

**Audience brief:** `Canadian homeowners aged 35-55, considering home renovation`
**Message A:** `Your dream kitchen is closer than you think. Free quote in 24 hours.`
**Message B:** `Stop overpaying for renovations. Compare 3 local contractors instantly.`

- **What to show:** Switch to A/B mode. Same panel evaluates both variants. Side-by-side comparison with preference percentages.
- **Point to make:** This isn't just sentiment — panelists pick a winner and explain why. You get the "why" behind the preference.
- **Look for in reactions:** Aspirational (A) vs. practical (B) framing. Higher-income panelists might prefer A; cost-conscious prefer B. Someone noting that "dream kitchen" is a cliché. "Compare 3 local contractors" raises trust questions — who are these contractors?

## SCENARIO 15: Survey Pre-Test (45s)

**Audience brief:** `Canadian parents with children in public school, K-12`
**Survey questions:**
1. `How satisfied are you with your child's school? (1-5)`
2. `Do you feel the curriculum adequately prepares your child for the future?`
3. `Would you support increased property taxes to fund smaller class sizes?`

- **What to show:** Switch to survey pre-test mode. Panelists evaluate each question for clarity, bias, and cultural sensitivity.
- **Point to make:** Question 3 is a classic leading question — watch the panel catch it. "Adequately" in Q2 is vague. Different cultural backgrounds interpret "satisfied" differently.
- **Look for in reactions:** Bias flags on Q3 (framing taxes as the only mechanism). Ambiguity flags on "adequately" and "the future." Cultural flags from immigrant parents with different education system expectations. A low-income parent noting that property tax increases hit renters too (via rent increases).

## PRODUCTION NOTES

- **Pacing:** Each scenario should feel snappy. Type the brief → show the panel assembling → highlight 2-3 interesting reactions → move on. Don't linger.
- **Screen recording:** Split-screen — input on left, reactions streaming on right. Show the SSE streaming effect (reactions appearing one by one).
- **Callouts:** Use on-screen text callouts to highlight interesting reaction quotes and cultural flags. Don't read everything — let viewers pause if they want to read.
- **Music:** Upbeat but not frantic. Think product demo energy, not hype video.
- **Transitions:** Quick cuts between scenarios. Use a running counter ("Scenario 7 of 15") to maintain momentum.
- **B-roll ideas:** Quick cuts of Canadian landscapes matching each scenario — Costco parking lot, Diwali lights, prairie farms, Toronto skyline, hockey rink.
- **Thumbnail:** Grid of diverse persona cards with the text "15 Scenarios, 0 Setup"
